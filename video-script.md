# More Than Technology — 3–5 Minute Production Script

**Student:** Claudia Casso  
**Course:** EDTC 6320  
**Audience:** Prospective Educational Technology students and potential employers

## Scene 1 — Opening | 0:00–0:20
**Narration:**  
When people hear “Educational Technology,” they often think of laptops, apps, or online courses. Those tools are visible. The design decisions behind effective learning are not. That is where Educational Technology begins.

**On-screen text:** More Than Technology

## Scene 2 — What is Educational Technology? | 0:20–0:50
**Narration:**  
Educational Technology is an ethical, research- and theory-informed field focused on improving learning and performance through intentional design, implementation, and evaluation. The goal is not to use technology simply because it is available. The goal is to choose the right process and resources for the learning need.

**Visual:** Learning need → design → resources → evaluation

## Scene 3 — What do Educational Technologists do? | 0:50–1:25
**Narration:**  
Educational Technologists analyze learners, identify performance gaps, design instruction, develop digital and multimedia resources, support accessibility, integrate technology, and evaluate results. They may create online courses, employee training, performance-support tools, faculty development, or technology-supported instruction.

**On-screen text:** Analyze • Design • Develop • Implement • Evaluate

## Scene 4 — Professional skills | 1:25–1:50
**Narration:**  
The work combines instructional design, learner analysis, multimedia development, accessibility, project management, communication, assessment, and evaluation. Technology is part of the skill set, but good design starts with the problem—not the tool.

## Scene 5 — Careers and settings | 1:50–2:20
**Narration:**  
Educational Technology professionals work in K–12 schools, colleges and universities, corporate learning, healthcare, government, nonprofit organizations, and military training. Job titles may include Instructional Designer, Learning Experience Designer, E-Learning Developer, Educational Technology Specialist, Training and Development Specialist, or Instructional Technology Coordinator.

## Scene 6 — Earnings | 2:20–2:45
**Narration:**  
There is no single Bureau of Labor Statistics category for every Educational Technology professional, so related occupations provide useful context. In May 2025, the U.S. median annual wage was about $69,280 for Training and Development Specialists, $77,440 for Instructional Coordinators, and $133,000 for Training and Development Managers. Earnings vary by role, industry, location, education, and experience.

## Scene 7 — History | 2:45–3:15
**Narration:**  
Educational Technology developed long before today’s digital tools. Visual and audiovisual instruction expanded teaching beyond print. Systematic training and instructional design emphasized objectives and performance. Computers, multimedia, the Internet, and online learning expanded access. Mobile learning, analytics, and artificial intelligence continue that evolution.

## Scene 8 — Theory and standards | 3:15–3:50
**Narration:**  
The profession is grounded in learning theory. Behaviorism emphasizes observable outcomes. Cognitivism focuses on memory and information processing. Constructivism emphasizes active, authentic learning. Multimedia-learning research and Universal Design for Learning help designers communicate clearly and reduce barriers. AECT professional standards connect these ideas to ethical, research-informed practice.

## Scene 9 — Closing | 3:50–4:10
**Narration:**  
Educational Technology is not simply about using technology. It is about understanding people, learning, and performance—and then designing the right solution. For students, that means a profession with many possible paths. For employers, it means a professional who can connect learning needs to purposeful design and measurable improvement.

**On-screen text:** Analyze the need. Design the experience. Improve the learning.
