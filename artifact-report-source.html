01 - Project Description

A professional introduction to the field

I developed More Than Technology to address a gap in how prospective students and potential employers understand Educational Technology. The project combines a public website with a 4:33 narrated multimedia presentation that explains the profession, professional responsibilities, career settings, related earnings information, historical development, learning theory, and AECT standards.

The design is grounded in audience analysis, multimedia-learning principles, Universal Design for Learning, accessibility, and authentic professional examples. The project began as a Google Sites version and was later redesigned and deployed through Vercel to provide greater control over the final instructional experience.

Primary audiences: prospective Educational Technology students and potential employers of Educational Technology professionals.

03 - Modifications Made

Why I moved the project from Google Sites to Vercel

The first working version was created in Google Sites. Google Sites was useful for developing the initial structure quickly because it provided straightforward page creation, built-in navigation, and simple publishing. As the project developed, however, the instructional design needed more control over how information was organized and presented to the two target audiences.

I moved the revised version to Vercel so I could build a custom static website with HTML and CSS and make the design choices match the instructional goals more closely. The change was not made because Google Sites was unusable. It was a deliberate redesign decision based on the needs of the project.

Stronger information architecture.
The Google version used many separate top-level pages. The Vercel redesign consolidated related proposal content so audience analysis, learning opportunity, goals, solution, and rationale read as one connected instructional-design argument.
Greater visual control.
Vercel allowed custom spacing, typography, cards, banners, section hierarchy, and consistent visual emphasis rather than relying primarily on a standard template.
Better responsive and accessibility control.
The custom site made it possible to directly manage semantic structure, keyboard-accessible navigation, contrast, mobile layouts, descriptive links, video caption presentation, and PDF viewing.
Improved multimedia integration.
The final site could more deliberately integrate the narrated video, caption file, AI-generated educational media, downloadable Artifact Report PDF, and media credits without making the page feel crowded.
Version 1 - Google Sites: https://sites.google.com/view/morethantechnology/home

Design result: Moving platforms allowed the final product to become more audience-centered, visually coherent, accessible, and easier to scan while preserving the same instructional purpose and research foundation.

06 - Professional Reflection

What this project changed in my understanding

This project reinforced that Educational Technology begins with people and problems, not tools. The strongest design decisions came from identifying what the audience needed to understand and then choosing a structure, media approach, and level of detail that supported that need. The move from Google Sites to Vercel made that lesson practical: the platform itself became a design decision because the final implementation needed to support the audience, not simply host the content.

I also developed a stronger appreciation for the connection between theory and practical design. Multimedia principles, UDL, accessibility, ethics, and professional standards become meaningful when they change the way information is organized, represented, and evaluated. A polished product is not enough if its claims are vague, unsupported, difficult to navigate, or inaccessible. As I continue through the M.Ed. Educational Technology program, I want to strengthen my ability to connect research to practice and evaluate instructional decisions more systematically.
