Client Proposal

A clearer introduction to Educational Technology

Designed for prospective students and potential employers who need an accurate, practical explanation of the field and the value of the professionals who work in it.

Project overview

The instructional need

Educational Technology is often reduced to devices, software, or technical support. That view misses the professional work that happens before and after a tool is selected: analyzing learners, clarifying performance needs, designing instruction, developing media, supporting accessibility, and evaluating outcomes.

More Than Technology addresses that misunderstanding through a public website and a concise 3–5 minute multimedia presentation. Together, they explain the profession, the range of work settings, related career information, historical and theoretical foundations, and the professional standards that guide practice.

Recommended solution

  • Public, mobile-friendly project website
  • 3–5 minute narrated multimedia presentation
  • Captions and transcript for the final video
  • Research-based design rationale
  • Artifact Report aligned to EDTC 6320 standards

Target audience

Who needs this information?

Prospective students

Potential learners may be educators, trainers, administrators, healthcare professionals, military personnel, corporate professionals, government employees, or career changers. They need a practical picture of what the degree can prepare them to do.

Potential employers

Hiring managers, education leaders, training leaders, healthcare organizations, corporations, government agencies, and military training environments need to understand how Educational Technology professionals contribute beyond basic technical support.

Learning opportunity

From limited awareness to informed understanding

Current reality

Stakeholders may recognize educational tools but not the work that happens around them. They may be unfamiliar with learner analysis, instructional design, learning theory, accessibility, performance improvement, multimedia development, evaluation, and professional standards.

Desired situation

Prospective students should be able to explain the profession, identify major responsibilities and career settings, and connect the degree to professional interests. Employers should be able to identify the contribution Educational Technology professionals can make to learning and performance problems.

The instructional gap exists between stakeholders’ limited understanding of Educational Technology and the knowledge needed to make informed educational, career, and hiring decisions.

Project goals

What viewers should be able to do

GOAL 01

Describe the work

Describe at least three major responsibilities of an Educational Technologist.

GOAL 02

Recognize the settings

Identify at least three professional environments or career areas in which Educational Technology skills may be applied.

GOAL 03

Connect the foundations

Explain how learning theory, instructional design, research, technology, and evaluation work together to improve learning and performance.

Instructional solution

A short multimedia experience supported by a deeper website

The multimedia presentation provides an efficient overview, while the website gives viewers a place to explore the reasoning, research, standards, and references behind the presentation.

The website-and-video combination was selected because the target audience includes working adults with varied professional backgrounds and different levels of prior knowledge about Educational Technology. The short multimedia format provides an efficient introduction, while the website allows viewers to explore the learner analysis, professional standards, research, and design decisions in greater depth. This combination supports flexible access and applies multimedia-learning principles by organizing information into manageable, visually supported segments.

Design rationale

Every design choice should serve learning

Design purpose: These instructional decisions are intended to support learning and enhance pedagogy by connecting theory, media, accessibility, and authentic professional examples. Because the audience may represent different cultures, careers, ages, abilities, educational experiences, and levels of technology familiarity, the project uses examples from multiple sectors and avoids presenting one cultural or professional context as the norm. This supports an inclusive view of learning within a diverse, multicultural community.

Multimedia learning

Mayer’s work informs the use of concise narration, relevant visuals, limited on-screen text, signaling, coherence, and segmentation. The purpose is to direct attention to essential ideas without competing information.

Universal Design for Learning

CAST’s UDL framework supports multiple ways to access the message. Captions, transcript, visual-verbal explanations, clear navigation, and flexible viewing help account for learner variability.

Authentic learning

Examples from real professional settings help viewers connect abstract descriptions of Educational Technology to recognizable problems in education, business, healthcare, government, and military training.

Audience-centered design

Busy viewers need concise information; unfamiliar viewers need plain language; diverse audiences need inclusive examples and accessible formats. Those learner characteristics directly shape the website and presentation.

Accessibility, diversity & ethics

Built into the design, not added later

The site uses semantic HTML, keyboard-accessible navigation, readable typography, sufficient contrast, responsive layouts, and descriptive link labels. The multimedia presentation includes captions and a separate caption file so viewers can access the content in more than one way.

The project also promotes health and safety through readable layouts, restrained motion, accessible contrast, and the avoidance of flashing or distracting visual effects that could interfere with viewing or accessibility.

Media, copyright, Fair Use & open access: media and research materials are selected with respect for copyright, Fair Use, Creative Commons licensing, public-domain materials, and appropriate open-access resources. Sources are credited, and search-engine image results are not treated as licenses. AI-generated media used in the project is disclosed on the Sources & Media Credits page.
